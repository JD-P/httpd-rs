Interpretation:

The requests alternated a nonexistent username and the configured username on
fresh loopback TCP connections after warming the parsed AuthUserFile cache.
Both failure classes returned byte-equivalent stable responses (Date excluded):
status 401, the same headers, and the same 318-byte body.  The configured-user
class nevertheless incurred the cost-12 bcrypt verifier and was 192.7x,
270.2x, and 251.1x slower by median in the three runs.  The successful-password control
returned the protected marker with status 200, proving that the measured
username names a live account rather than a dead fixture.

Rejected hypotheses from the same pass:

- Repeated RST_STREAM does not consume the vendored h2 control-frame bucket,
  but the universal frame-work limiter charges four units per reset and caps
  it at a 131,072-unit burst / 65,536-unit-per-second refill. Pending-accept
  reset streams have an additional limit. This was retained as hardening debt,
  not inflated into a vulnerability claim.
- A source audit suggested MultiViews negotiation headers were appended after
  ordinary Header policy. A live Apache 2.4.69 control also retained
  Content-Location/TCN and combined its negotiation Vary with `Header set
  Vary Origin`, so the proposed security differential was rejected.
